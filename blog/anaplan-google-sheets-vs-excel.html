Anaplan in Google Sheets vs Excel: aPlan4Sheets alongside Anaplan XL for Mac and Chromebook teams

Anaplan XL Reporting (AXL, formerly Fluence XL) is Anaplan's own add-in for Excel. aPlan4Sheets brings the same Anaplan models into Google Sheets. Here is how to decide which spreadsheet each report should connect from, and when a team needs both.

Two spreadsheets, one Anaplan model

Most Anaplan customers report from more than one spreadsheet platform. Anaplan XL serves the Excel side: it is Anaplan's supported add-in for desktop Excel and Microsoft 365 workflows. aPlan4Sheets serves the Google Workspace side: it runs inside Google Sheets in the browser, so Mac, PC, Chromebook, and Linux users work from the same shared workbook with no desktop software to install.

The question is rarely which tool is better. It is which spreadsheet your reports, reviewers, and controls already live in. Keep Anaplan as the single source and connect from the spreadsheet each team actually uses.

When aPlan4Sheets is the right fit

  • Your team uses Google Workspace, and reports are reviewed in shared Google Sheets
  • You have Mac or Chromebook users who need Anaplan data in a spreadsheet
  • You want collaborators to work from one shared workbook that refreshes on a configured cadence
  • You want hourly, daily, or weekly refreshes configured in the sidebar, with status you can check
  • You want to build Google Sheets charts and Google Slides reporting from connected data, with a review step

When Excel and Anaplan XL are the right choice

  • Your team works in desktop Excel and Microsoft 365
  • You have existing Excel workbooks, macros, or reports built around Anaplan XL
  • Your organization standardizes spreadsheet work on Microsoft 365
  • You want Anaplan's own supported add-in for Excel

Running both

Many organizations end up with both: Anaplan XL for finance users who build in Excel, and aPlan4Sheets for business partners and leadership reviews that happen in Google Sheets. Because both connect to the same Anaplan models, the numbers come from one place; only the spreadsheet in front of the reader changes.
