About

Built by a planner who needed Anaplan in Google Sheets

aPlan4Sheets started as an internal tool for a finance team that planned in Anaplan and reported in Google Workspace. It grew into a Google Workspace Marketplace add-on because other teams had the same gap: Anaplan's own spreadsheet add-in is built for Excel, and their analysts worked on Macs, Chromebooks and shared Google Sheets.

What it does

The connector brings saved views, exports, module views and formatted UX pages from an Anaplan model into Google Sheets, keeps them refreshable by hand or on a schedule, and lets analysts repivot, link and clone their reporting setup. An AI Analyst answers questions about the model and its data with a review step before anything is exported to a sheet. Every request runs under the user's own Anaplan permissions.

It does not write back to Anaplan. The security and data-handling page describes what the add-on can reach, what it stores, and where.

Who builds it

aPlan4Sheets is designed, built and supported by Ryan Domke, an FP&A practitioner and Anaplan model builder. The product decisions come from day-to-day planning work: month-end variance packs, board reporting, headcount and revenue models, and the reconciliations that sit between a planning model and the spreadsheet a leadership team actually reads.

Support goes to the person who wrote the code. Questions, setup help and feedback reach ryan.domke@aplan4sheets.com.

Where it stands

  • Listed on the Google Workspace Marketplace since 2025 and used in production for recurring finance reporting.
  • Released continuously. Autumn 2026 additions include linked pivots and combined module grids, exact nested selections, formatted UX page exports, row grouping by hierarchy, the AI Analyst with a review step, and sturdier scheduled refreshes.
  • Runs entirely on Google infrastructure: an Apps Script sidebar inside Sheets and a connector service in Google Cloud. No desktop software, no third-party hosting.
  • Every walkthrough on this site is an interactive simulation with fictional data, so nothing here comes from a customer model.
