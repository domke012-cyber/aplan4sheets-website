How to Connect Anaplan to Google Sheets

A step-by-step guide to pulling Anaplan data into Google Sheets with the aPlan4Sheets add-on. It runs in the browser, so there is no desktop software to install.

Why connect Anaplan to Google Sheets?

Anaplan is the standard for enterprise planning, but many FP&A teams still need data in spreadsheets for ad-hoc analysis, board reporting, and collaboration with stakeholders who do not have Anaplan access.

For Excel-centred teams, Anaplan XL Reporting (AXL, formerly Fluence XL) is Anaplan's own add-in. aPlan4Sheets covers the same need for teams whose reporting lives in Google Workspace: it brings Anaplan data directly into Google Sheets through the browser, so it works wherever Google Sheets does, including Mac and Chromebook.

What you'll need

  • A Google Workspace account on your organization's domain. The OAuth/SSO sign-in flow does not support Gmail or personal-domain accounts.
  • An Anaplan account with access to the workspace and model you want to connect
  • A modern browser such as Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge
  • Approval from your Google Workspace administrator, if your organization reviews add-on installations

Step 1: Install aPlan4Sheets

Visit the Google Workspace Marketplace listing and click "Install". Review the requested permissions and follow your organization's approval process. The add-on then appears in the Google Sheets Extensions menu. A Google Workspace administrator can also install it domain-wide from the Admin Console.

Step 2: Sign in to Anaplan

Open any Google Sheet and go to Extensions > aPlan4Sheets > Open Sidebar. The sidebar opens on a Sign-in method selector. Leave it set to OAuth/SSO and click Sign in with Anaplan SSO; a popup opens so you can complete your organization's login. If your organization permits it, you can switch the method to Basic Auth and sign in with your Anaplan email and password instead.

OAuth/SSO uses Anaplan's authentication flow and avoids entering an Anaplan password in the connector. If your tenant has not created an OAuth client yet, a tenant security administrator creates it in Anaplan Administration; the setup guide walks through it. Basic Auth retains your Anaplan email and password in user-specific Google Apps Script storage. See the Privacy Policy for storage and retention details.

Step 3: Add a model

After signing in, the sidebar reads "No models configured yet". Click Add Model, select the tenant, choose an available workspace, select the model you are authorized to access, and click Save Model. You can add more than one model; the sidebar then lists the connected model's export actions, saved views, and pivot views.

Step 4: Export or pivot your data

The sidebar has three ways to bring data into the sheet:

  • Export Actions: Open the Export Actions card, select one or more exports defined in the model, and click Run Selected Exports. Review the destination warning before confirming; existing data in the destination can be replaced.
  • Saved Views: Open the Saved Views card, select a view, and click Export View. The view lands in the sheet as configured in Anaplan, with no manual mapping.
  • Pivot Views: Select a view and click Configure Pivot. Arrange dimensions across rows, columns, and pages, select the page items you want, and click Create Pivot to write the layout into the sheet. Use Repivot later to reopen the saved pivot with updated settings.

Supported UX grid widgets can also be connected, with their grid styles, such as hierarchy emphasis, number formats, and conditional colors, brought into Sheets.

Step 5: Refresh on demand or on a schedule

Click Refresh Sheets to update the configurations you select. To automate updates, open Scheduled Refreshes, click + Add Schedule, choose the scope and a cadence (hourly, daily, or weekly), and click Save Schedule. Google-managed trigger timing is approximate, and each run depends on valid authentication and the source model being available; the sidebar shows status and errors when a refresh needs attention.

Works on every platform

Because aPlan4Sheets runs in the browser through Google Sheets, it works on Mac, PC, Chromebook, and Linux. There is no desktop software to install.
